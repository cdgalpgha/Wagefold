# Payroll buyers want control, not more features

The loudest complaint about payroll products, crypto or fiat, is money that is held, frozen, or "sent" but never arrives, with no explanation. Close behind it come payroll runs that pay the wrong amount, the wrong person, or nobody, because whoever approved them could not see what had changed. Feature gaps rank lower. For Wagefold this is good news, because its core design already answers the top two complaints. Paying non-custodially, wallet to wallet, removes the platform-side KYC holds and frozen balances behind the largest cluster of 1-star reviews. The "changes since last payroll" review answers the failure that caused the best-documented on-chain payroll loss. What Wagefold lacks is the layer finance teams and DAO treasurers treat as table stakes before they will move a real treasury. That layer is approvals by more than one person, inside the Safe they already use, plus records an accountant can close the month with and a recipient can show a lender. The evidence points to this build order: Safe-native multi-signer execution, then roles with maker-checker approval and wallet-change control, then accounting and tax exports, then payment statements (payslips), then scheduled runs with reminders that always stop for a human approval. One caveat governs everything below. **Reddit could not be accessed by the researchers.** It was blocked at the search, fetch and API level. So this report rests on Hacker News comments, DAO governance forums, Trustpilot reviews, G2 and Capterra snippets, and practitioner and vendor guides, and none of it should be read as Reddit sentiment.

## Reddit was unreachable, so the evidence is HN, DAO forums and review sites

Every research thread hit the same wall. WebSearch refused reddit.com, WebFetch refused old.reddit.com, direct requests returned 403, and archive mirrors either failed or block AI agents. **No Reddit quotes appear anywhere in this report.** The substitutes were about 166 Hacker News comments pulled through the Algolia API (roughly 32 relevant first- or second-hand accounts on crypto pay, plus about 45 on general SMB payroll), and full threads from the ENS, Gitcoin, Arbitrum, Optimism, Balancer and Decentraland governance forums. The rest came from Trustpilot pages for Rise, Bitwage, Request Finance, Deel, Remote, Gusto, Papaya, Oyster, Rippling, Wise and Payoneer, and from search-snippet extracts of G2 and Capterra, whose review pages returned 403. The counts quoted below are counts of what the researchers read, not population estimates. Trustpilot over-represents angry users, and recent-first pages lean negative. Much of the finance and compliance material comes from vendors (Bitwave, Acuity, Toku, Rise), and it is flagged as vendor material where it is used.

The ranking that follows combines how often each pain recurs across these sources, how much money it costs when it happens, and how many buyer types it touches.

## Held money and silent failures dominate every complaint list

The single most consistent finding across all five research threads is that people forgive a slow feature and do not forgive money they cannot reach. Rise, the crypto payout platform with the largest review base, scores 4.3 on Trustpilot. Behind that average sits a **14% 1-star tail across 8,031 reviews, roughly 1,100 people**. On the first page, about 6 reviews cite stuck or rejected KYC and about 7 cite blocked funds, in lines like "They have my funds and I'm unable to access them" and "7 weeks, 5 support requests, the same canned reply, and still no account" ([Trustpilot: Rise](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/riseworks.io)). That review base mixes payroll payees with prop-trading payout recipients. The pattern still holds, because the complaint is about the platform holding money. The same shape shows up in mainstream global payroll. Deel blocked one paycheck because "my account was passing through a compliance evaluation" ([Trustpilot: Deel 1★](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/deel.com?stars=1)). Remote did not even notify a contractor whose payout had failed ([Trustpilot: Remote](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/remote.com)). An Oyster reviewer wrote: "They hold wages, even though the company has paid them" ([Trustpilot: Oyster](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/oysterhr.com)). Payoneer had "frozen my $861.62 and blocked me from withdrawing my own money" ([Trustpilot: Payoneer](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/payoneer.com)). Gusto "withdrew wages from employee's own bank accounts and blocked our payroll access" ([Trustpilot: Gusto](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/gusto.com)). Bitwage, which dates from 2014, draws a smaller cluster of "payment is delayed 4 days already and no support at all" ([Trustpilot: Bitwage](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/bitwage.com)).

What turns a hold into a 1-star review is silence. Reviewers seem to accept a delay when they are told why, for how long, and what to do. The anger in these reviews comes from templated replies, contradictory answers ("They lie, they mislead, they give you contradictory information" ([Trustpilot: Deel 1★](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/deel.com?stars=1))) and no escalation path ("I asked to speak to a supervisor and was told there are no supervisors" ([Trustpilot: Gusto](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/gusto.com))). Support was the most frequent complaint theme in the general SMB sample, about 7 of 17 recent Gusto reviews ([Trustpilot: Gusto](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/gusto.com?sort=recency)). Wagepoint's CEO claims that "ninety percent" of small businesses that switch payroll providers do so over service ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=1231748)), but that is a vendor executive's claim, not a survey. Fees follow the same rule. Users praise Papaya for being "accurate to the exchange rate estimation" ([Trustpilot: Papaya](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/papayaglobal.com)) and Wise for "zero hidden markups" ([Trustpilot: Wise](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/wise.com)). They complain bitterly about Deel's "unagreed fees" ([Trustpilot: Deel 1★](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/deel.com?stars=1)), and a happy Bitwage customer still wished fee information were easier to find ([Trustpilot: Bitwage](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/bitwage.com)).

This is where Wagefold's architecture earns its keep. A non-custodial tool that sends from the employer's wallet straight to the recipient's wallet cannot freeze a balance, because it never holds one. That removes the platform-side failure behind the biggest complaint category in the data. It does not remove KYC holds at the recipient's own exchange, and the pitch has to say so honestly: "we never hold your team's money" is true, while "cashing out is painless" is not. The second implication is subtler. Wagefold already verifies payments on-chain on the server, so it holds the facts every one of these complainants lacked: whether the payment confirmed, when, and with which transaction hash. Showing that status to the recipient as well as the payer, with a failure notice when a run reverts, turns the "sent but not arrived" complaint into a link anyone can check. It is cheap to build, and it is the most direct answer to rank 1.

## Payroll losses come from what signers could not see

The documented on-chain payroll losses share one root cause: the people approving the run could not see what was different this time. In March 2023, PeopleDAO built its payroll from an edit-enabled spreadsheet that had been shared publicly. An attacker added and hid a row paying about 76 ETH (~$120k). The batch went through Safe's CSV Airdrop, and "6 out of 9 multisig signers did not notice the malicious transfer" among roughly 80 transfers ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2023-03-13-peopledao-hacked-via-google-sheets-219214)). Post-mortem coverage noted there was "no rollup showing total payments that could have flagged it" ([Web3 is Going Great](https://web3isgoinggreat.com/single/peopledao-loses-120000-after-payment-spreadsheet-is-shared-publicly)). In December 2024, a Questbook-administered Arbitrum grant paid impersonators because KYC matched "a Raphael ... [who] is not the same person as the Raphael that was mentioned in the proposal" ([Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/questbook-dda-program-report/27859/7)). In August 2025, ENS DAO's Superfluid relayer failed to refill its buffer, streams dried up, service providers were cut off mid-month, and the DAO needed an emergency proposal to process **400k USDCx in backpay** ([ENS forum](https://discuss.ens.domains/t/ens-streamer-optimized-payments-streaming/21652/1)). That account comes from a competing builder's proposal, so it is not neutral. Fiat payroll has the same failure mode: one engineer was "accidentally paid approximately a whole years salary at once" in the first run after an acquisition ([HN 48873470](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48873470)).

Fraud adds deliberate attacks on that blind spot. Address poisoning seeds a lookalike of a frequent recipient into a wallet's history, so that a copy-paste from history pays the attacker. Payroll is exactly the target profile: the same addresses, paid on predictable dates. Chainalysis tracked one campaign of **82,031 lookalike addresses** between February and May 2024, in which **2,774 victims sent $69.7M**. One victim sent about $68M in WBTC to an address that matched only the first six characters, minutes after a legitimate test payment ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/address-poisoning-scam/)). In late 2025 an attacker watched a $50 test transfer, planted a spoof, and caught a 49,999,950 USDT payment about 26 minutes later ([CoinGape](https://coingape.com/nearly-50m-in-usdt-stolen-after-address-poisoning-scam/)). That case shows that a test payment protects nothing if the main payment re-copies the address. Payroll diversion is the fiat analogue: attackers take over an employee's email, change the deposit details, and add mailbox rules that hide the change alerts ([FBI notification](https://www.lehman.cuny.edu/itr/documents/FBI-notification.pdf)). A crypto payment, unlike an ACH, cannot be recalled. At the largest scale, Bybit lost about **$1.46B in February 2025 on valid signatures from 3 of 6 signers**, because the transaction shown in the Safe interface was not the one being signed ([Curvegrid](https://www.curvegrid.com/blog/2025-03-18-the-bybit-safe-wallet-hack)). Certora calls the failure "blind signing" ([Certora](https://certora.com/blog/bybit-hack-multisig-wallet-security)), and Hacken concludes the common thread is "inadequate operational security, signer workflows, and transaction verification tools", not flaws in the contracts ([Hacken Q1 2025](https://wp.hacken.io/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Q1-2025-Security-Report-1.pdf)). The exact attack mechanism is disputed between those analyses, but they agree the signers could not verify what they signed.

The researchers found no verified 2024–2026 case of a startup or DAO payroll lost specifically to address poisoning or a fake "update my wallet" request. That gap probably reflects Reddit and X being unreachable rather than the attack never happening. The controls the evidence supports are still clear. Pay only from a verified payee directory, never from wallet history. Show the full address. Flag new and changed wallets. Show the batch total and each person's change against the last run. Put any wallet change through confirmation on a separate channel, an approval, and a cooling-off period before it can be paid. Give signers a decoded, human-readable payload they can check against an independently computed hash. Wagefold's "changes since last payroll" view already implements the first half of that list, and it maps almost exactly onto the PeopleDAO post-mortem. It is the product's strongest differentiator. The missing half is the workflow that governs how a wallet gets changed, and a second pair of eyes before the run executes.

## Recipients want spendable local money with paperwork, not just USDC

Contractors and DAO contributors have a different job to get done than the people paying them. Off-ramping is the dominant recipient theme, in about 10 of the ~32 relevant HN comments. A Malaysian worker reported "a 10% drop in value from USDC to MYR" ([HN 42063819](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42063819)). Another commenter's friend "literally travels to another country to withdraw what he is paid in crypto" ([HN 48641202](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48641202)). A Canadian contractor described a five-hop Deel → Wise → Interac → bank → Interac → Wealthsimple chain, and said they would rather take USDT or USDC directly if their brokerage accepted it ([HN 47496728](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47496728)). A commenter who is paid in crypto summed it up: "the most expensive and painful parts of my finances are those that touch the traditional banking system at all" ([HN 39047638](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39047638)). Volatility barely comes up once pay is in stablecoins, and some recipients actively prefer them. Senders report contractors in Nigeria and the Philippines who "want to get paid in USDT. It's instant and there is a thriving local scene of P2P sellers" ([HN 45133380](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45133380)). Pantera's 2024 compensation survey found that **USDC made up 63% of crypto salaries and USDT 28.6%**, and that none of Deel, Remote or Rippling offered USDT for payroll ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/crypto-salaries-triple-2024-stablecoins-payrolls-report)). The DAO side is more conservative. At ENS, "no DAO contributor has been paid in anything other than USDC", and a proposal to switch part of the treasury to a newer stablecoin drew delegate opposition over liquidity and complexity ([ENS forum](https://discuss.ens.domains/t/temp-check-convert-20-of-ens-daos-usdc-holdings-to-glo-dollar-usdglo-to-fund-web3-public-goods-at-no-cost/19667/17)).

Wagefold already supports USDC and USDT. The open question is chain. The notes do not say which network USDT-preferring recipients use, though one researcher infers it is Tron, which a Base-only product cannot serve. Recording each payee's chosen token, confirmed by the recipient, is still worth doing. In Rise's own explanation, one stuck withdrawal "was caused by my funds being paid in USD while my withdrawal method was configured for GBP" ([Trustpilot IE: Rise](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/www.riseworks.io)). That is the kind of route mismatch a recipient-confirmed preference prevents.

The second recipient need is legitimacy. Crypto pay carries a stigma: one Upwork freelancer treats a client's crypto offer as "a red flag" for laundering ([HN 35839379](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=35839379)), one commenter lists people "paid in crypto" among those who get debanked ([HN 50152258](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50152258)), and another had to produce documents proving crypto income for a source-of-funds check ([HN 44979955](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44979955)). The direct demand for proof of income is quiet but real. One Request Finance reviewer valued that it lets them "receive my salary in cryptocurrency and have proof of my income" ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/accounting/request-finance-profile/), snippet). A founder paying a single foreign worker wanted to "do it properly ... generating a paystub" ([HN 38470233](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38470233)). Indian guidance tells crypto-paid workers of foreign employers to keep "contracts, payslips, wallet addresses, and valuation records" ([Goodreturns](https://www.goodreturns.in/personal-finance/investment/using-stablecoins-for-salary-payments-reporting-crypto-income-under-current-tax-codes-1430927.html)). US mortgage lenders typically want W-2s or 1099s and **the most recent 30 days of pay stubs with year-to-date earnings** ([Opendoor](https://www.opendoor.com/articles/proof-of-income-for-a-mortgage)). A transaction hash on its own does not meet that bar. An employer-issued statement showing the fiat value at payment, the pay period, the YTD total and the transaction hash probably does, though no source confirms how lenders treat USDC income. Because Wagefold pays people directly and does not withhold tax, this is better framed as a "payment statement" than a W-2-style payslip.

## Finance teams buy approvals, exports and an audit trail

Finance buyers, founders and DAO treasurers describe the same must-haves in different words. All of them want automation that prepares the run, with a human who approves before money moves. Founders on HN praise "zero clicks" payroll ([HN 48134131](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48134131)) but draw a hard line at unattended payouts: "In no world would I give Claude or any AI agent direct write access to financial operations like payouts/settlements" ([HN 48131365](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48131365)). Review steps pay for themselves; one founder recalls accountants who "made $100K+ mistakes if we hadn't caught them" ([HN 45061692](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45061692)). Vendor-authored control checklists ask for role-based access, segregation of duties, wallets verified "against your allow-list", and wallet changes that "require explicit approval with immutable audit trails" ([Bitwave](https://www.bitwave.io/blog/real-time-reconciliation-reporting-for-stablecoin-payouts-2025-guide)). Roles go wrong in practice too: Rippling "placed one of my employees as the super admin without my knowledge" ([Trustpilot: Rippling](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/rippling.com)). For DAOs and treasuries, the approver is a multisig. One vendor describes the default "onchain payroll system" as "a Safe multisig combined with Google Sheets", where treasury managers "chase signers for approval" every month ([Sablier blog](https://blog.sablier.com/crypto-payroll-tools-compared-sablier-vs-traditional-solutions)). Coordination is expensive. Arbitrum's Multisig Support Service ran eight multisigs, including a dedicated payroll multisig on a 9-of-12 setup, for **$234k a year** ([Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/wind-down-the-mss-transfer-payment-responsibilities-to-the-arbitrum-foundation/29279/1)). Signers there are handed "the name of the recipient alongside the wallet address" after KYC is done off-chain ([Arbitrum forum #4](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/wind-down-the-mss-transfer-payment-responsibilities-to-the-arbitrum-foundation/29279/4)). A Wagefold run that arrives in the Safe queue with that pairing and the diff already attached would replace this process outright.

Records are the second gate. Stablecoin accounting is unsettled. Forvis Mazars concludes that fiat-backed stablecoins "do not meet the accounting definition of cash or cash equivalents", and that depending on redemption rights they may be a receivable or an intangible asset ([Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.us/forsights/2025/11/accounting-for-stablecoins-navigating-uncertainty-within-us-gaap)). One CPA warns that "the biggest misconception in the U.S. is that a stablecoin is the same as cash" ([Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/stablecoin-payroll-gains-momentum-but-irs-rules-pose-compliance-challenges/)), and USDC briefly traded at $0.87 during the SVB failure in March 2023 ([Acuity](https://acuity.co/stablecoin-payroll/)). So an export should record the facts (token, chain, amount, USD rate with its source and timestamp, network fee, tx hash, from and to addresses, payee, pay period, approvers) and leave the accounting treatment to the accountant, rather than hard-coding $1.00. On tax, stablecoin wages are W-2 wages in the US. Contractor payments go on a 1099-NEC, with the threshold rising from $600 to **$2,000 starting in 2026** ([Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/stablecoin-payroll-gains-momentum-but-irs-rules-pose-compliance-challenges/)), and foreign payees need a W-8BEN on file ([Acuity](https://acuity.co/stablecoin-payroll/)). Annual per-payee USD totals are therefore a cheap, high-value report. In the general SMB evidence, QuickBooks compatibility is effectively required, because "some places won't audit without Quickbooks" ([HN 43417076](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43417076)). "You cannot get tabular reports in a spreadsheet" is a recurring complaint ([Capterra: Gusto](https://www.capterra.com/p/131882/Gusto/reviews/?page=73), snippet). Gusto's own guidance against syncing payroll into closed periods shows the posting discipline accountants expect ([Gusto Help Center](https://support.gusto.com/article/100971895100000/integrate-with-quickbooks-online)). A vendor roundup puts the stakes plainly: if payouts "cannot be mapped to invoices and exported cleanly," stablecoin payroll stays a permanent "special process" at month-end ([Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/best-stablecoin-payroll-tools/)).

Two more buyer concerns shape priorities. The first is scheduling. The market leader's own launch messaging promises "Run payroll for me every Friday" with anomaly flags ([HN 48371277](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48371277)), and Gusto users are angry when payroll is blocked "after the submission deadline siting insufficient funds" ([Trustpilot: Gusto](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/gusto.com?sort=recency)). The ENS/Superfluid outage shows the cost of payments that depend on a single unattended relayer. Taken together, this argues for schedules that prepare the run, check the treasury balance before payday and send reminders, but never execute without an approval. The second is durability. Utopia Labs, which Gitcoin had proposed for all of its DAO payments ([Gitcoin forum](https://gov.gitcoin.co/t/proposal-gitcoin-dao-budgeting-process-redesign/12493/1)), stopped service with about a month's notice in 2023 ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/utopia-labs-business-model-shift-sunset)), and Bitwage was reportedly acquired in 2025 ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/bitwage-vs-request-finance)). Toku's own buyer guide, which is vendor-authored, tells buyers to test "exit and data export" ([Toku](https://www.toku.com/resources/crypto-payroll-software)). On-chain history plus a full export is a credible answer to "what happens if you disappear".

## Conclusion

The research reframes what Wagefold is selling. The crypto payroll category has almost no employer-side reviews. Outside Rise's mostly payee-driven volume, most products have between 0 and about 160 reviews ([Trustpilot: Bitwage](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.bitwage.com); [G2: Coinshift](https://www.g2.com/sellers/coinshift)). Meanwhile the incumbents treat stablecoins as an opt-in withdrawal method run by partners: Deel with MoonPay, Papaya with Fireblocks, Remote with Stripe on Base. The researchers found no user reviews or uptake data for any of these ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/deel-partners-with-moonpay-to-enable-stablecoin-salary-payouts-for-global-workers-302683797.html); [Remote support](https://support.remote.com/hc/en-us/articles/32057660588429-How-can-contractors-receive-stablecoin-payouts)). The opening is not "stablecoin payroll exists". It is "stablecoin payroll that a controller can approve, an auditor can trace, and an attacker cannot quietly redirect." Speed is already commoditized on cheap L2s, and the evidence shows trust is decided by holds, errors and what approvers can see. Wagefold's non-custodial model and its changes review are the right foundation, and the next builds should give that foundation more approvers and more records rather than more payment features.

There are also clear things not to build. Custodial balances and an in-house off-ramp would bring back the KYC holds and frozen funds that generate the category's worst reviews. Streaming payroll inherits the relayer fragility seen at ENS. W-2 withholding needs a fiat payroll provider for tax remittance, which makes contractor and DAO-contributor pay the realistic market. Recipients' off-ramp pain is real, but Wagefold can address it with honest positioning, per-payee token and route confirmation, and statements that make the money legible to banks, without taking custody. The evidence is thinnest on founders' first-hand views of reconciliation, wallet collection at startups, and willingness to pay. Those are exactly the topics most likely to live on the Reddit communities the researchers could not reach, and they are worth testing in direct customer interviews before pricing or export formats are finalized.
